01why i want this

The class gave me a space. I want to keep it going.

The biggest thing this class gave me wasn't a tool or a technique — it was dedicated space, time, and community for working with this scary AI stuff. That's the part I'd love to help foster for the next cohort.

I went through an up-and-down journey with AI doom before landing somewhere genuinely optimistic, through this class. And as a woman software engineer learning new tech, I've held both feelings at once: very qualified, and very much an imposter. Funny how those two can coexist, right?

Things you don't understand can scare you. But if you work with them, they get less scary — and you start to see what they can do for you, rather than take from you.

This was my motivation for taking your class, and it's why I believe in what you are building: techno-optimism, learned through community. I'd love to help support folks who are sitting in the scared part right now, because I know exactly what that feels like.

02the evidence

I've been helping out already!

Technical help

  • Helped a classmate turn a prompt-driven data workflow into a saved, rerunnable script — moving the AI from runtime to build time, so it's cheaper and deterministic
  • Shared how to make a progressive web app you can install to your phone, and where to find free hosting
  • Showed folks how to ask the agent for automated regression tests so old bugs stay fixed

Community

  • Frequently shared resources and events in Discord — Matt Pocock's engineering skill library, Rundown AI's community bulletin board, Zo Computer's community vibe-coding event
  • I genuinely enjoy talking with people about their projects — and about their relationship with AI

Engineering wisdom

  • Flagged the classic pitfalls for classmates: scope bloat, and too much planning without enough building & iterating
  • Tried out classmates' projects and gave hands-on user-testing feedback

Since the curriculum has a lot of students who've never written code, having more engineers on call fills a real gap — the kind of debugging that unblocks someone in five minutes instead of derailing their whole week.

04what i took away

The class changed how I build — here's the trail it left.

  • ·Used a bunch of new AI and SWE tooling — AI agent ecosystems like Zo, Claude Code, Copilot, and Codex. Deployment infra including Cloudflare, Zo, and Github actions + Pages.
  • ·Compiled a personal skills library — github.com/j-alicia-long/skill-library — portable AI skills that work across tools and projects.
  • ·Learned to apply structured engineering practices to AI development — via skills like Matt Pocock's /grill-me, which interrogates a plan before any code gets written.
    • Built a web-config repo — my own AGENTS template files, pre-commit hooks, linters, and the rest — so every new project starts with good defaults instead of a blank page.
  • ·Started personalizing my productivity tools — like a custom Todo app, and using Obsidian as a personal notes vault.
  • ·Explored ways to track and optimize my AI usage — which turned into the spend tracker and carbon footprint projects above.
  • ·Feeling a lot more organized — and inspired to build!
05the role

What I'd actually do, week to week.

  • a.Async-first responder on Discord — helping answer questions and unblock student projects. Assist live in class when my schedule allows.
  • b.Coach students on best practices — how to align on an idea with your agent, how to find the right tools for your goals, and how to just start building.
  • c.More structure in class comms — polls, sharing threads, celebrating small wins. The community improves the class experience, and a little scaffolding makes it even easier for quieter folks to join in.
  • d.Share the journey itself — the doom-to-empowered arc, the imposter feelings, and what changed. Sometimes the most useful thing a TA can say is "I felt that too, and here's what helped."
~2–3 hrs/week, alongside my day job
